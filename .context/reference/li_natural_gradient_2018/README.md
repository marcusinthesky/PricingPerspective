# Natural Gradient via Optimal Transport

## Summary

Li and Montufar establish a Wasserstein statistical manifold by pulling
transport geometry onto parametrized probability models, showing that the
Wasserstein metric incorporates the ground metric of the sample space into
natural-gradient methods.

## Key findings

### F1 — Wasserstein geometry retains the ground-space metric

- Claim: Unlike Fisher information geometry, the Wasserstein metric on a
  statistical manifold inherits the metric structure of the underlying sample
  space, so distances between probability laws reflect the cost of moving mass
  through that space.
- Evidence: The paper constructs the Wasserstein statistical manifold and
  derives natural-gradient descent using the L2-Wasserstein metric tensor on
  parametrized families.
- Scope: The application is to statistical inference and optimization on
  probability models, not to economic covariance restrictions.

## Assessment

Cite as an intellectual ancestor for treating probability laws as geometric
objects whose distances depend on the ground space. Paper 1 uses this
perspective for distribution-valued firm characteristics in a semantic
embedding space, connected to systematic exposures through a maintained
bi-Lipschitz transmission map rather than through natural-gradient inference.
