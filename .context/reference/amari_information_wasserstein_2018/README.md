# Information Geometry Connecting Wasserstein Distance and Kullback--Leibler Divergence via the Entropy-Relaxed Transportation Problem

## Summary

Amari, Karakida, and Oizumi connect Wasserstein distance and KL divergence
through entropy-relaxed transportation, making explicit the distinction
between invariant information geometry and transport-based geometry on
probability laws.

## Key findings

### F1 — Wasserstein and KL divergence reflect different geometric structures

- Claim: Classical information geometry based on KL divergence is invariant
  to reparametrization, while Wasserstein geometry depends on the metric
  structure of the underlying sample space; the entropy-relaxed transport
  problem interpolates between them.
- Evidence: The paper derives the entropy-relaxed formulation and shows how
  the two geometries produce different notions of distance, curvature, and
  projection on families of probability laws.
- Scope: The framework concerns statistical divergences and does not address
  economic transmission maps or systematic covariance.

## Assessment

Cite as an early foundational paper connecting Wasserstein distance and
information geometry. The distinction it draws — that Wasserstein geometry
retains the base-space metric while KL geometry does not — motivates Paper 1's
use of semantic embedding geometry as a metrically meaningful characteristic
space for distribution-valued firm representations.
