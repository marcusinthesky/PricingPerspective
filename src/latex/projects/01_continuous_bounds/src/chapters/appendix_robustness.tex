\section{Empirical robustness and diagnostics}
\label{sec:empirical-robustness}

This appendix first audits the dyadic estimator and inference, then turns to
representation and finite article sampling, and finally considers the sector
projection and exploratory return-path display used in \Cref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Dyadic specification and inference audit}
\label{sec:dyadic-diagnostics}
\label{sec:resampling-formulas}

For the reported coefficient, let $X$ and $D$ stack $X_{ij}$ and $D_{ij}$ over
the observed dyads $i<j$, and let $F$ collect the intercept and one indicator
per non-omitted firm from the rank-cleaned point design. This nuisance design
is distinct from the secondary-control vector $z_{ij}$ in
equation~\ref{eq:dyadic-fixed-effects}, which is zero in the canonical
specification. Write $M_F=I-F(F^{\top}F)^{-}F^{\top}$, with
$\widetilde X=M_F X$ and $\widetilde D=M_F D$. The
Frisch--Waugh--Lovell identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fwl-identity}
  \widehat\beta_X
  =\frac{\widetilde X^{\top}\widetilde D}{\widetilde X^{\top}\widetilde X}.
\end{equation}
In words, both axes are first stripped of their average firm-endpoint
components. The remaining slope therefore measures whether unusually distant
article distributions for a given pair coincide with unusually weak return
co-movement for that pair.

The nuisance block has \ppvalue{confound-fwl-nuisance-columns} columns and
omits the indicator for \texttt{\ppvalue{confound-fwl-omitted-firm}}.

The conditional, or partial, $R^2$ of the article-embedding regressor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial-r2}
  R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}
  =1-\frac{\mathrm{SSE}_{\mathrm{FE}+X}}{\mathrm{SSE}_{\mathrm{FE}}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SSE}_{\mathrm{FE}}$ is the residual sum of squares from the
intercept and symmetric firm effects, and $\mathrm{SSE}_{\mathrm{FE}+X}$ is
from that model with $X$ added. It is defined only for the restricted
fixed-effect model and is therefore omitted on pooled rungs.
For the classification-adjusted estimates in
\Cref{tab:classification-controls}, replace $F$ by the joint nuisance block
containing firm effects and the included matching indicators or ETNIC
similarity.
The corresponding numerator and denominator in \eqref{eq:partial-r2} retain
those controls, so the partial $R^2$ isolates the contribution of $X$.
The within-industry fit uses the same construction on its restricted graph;
redundant endpoint indicators in disconnected industry groups are removed
by rank selection.

The finer classification fits in \Cref{tab:classification-controls} support
the sector-adjusted estimate.
Adding the same-industry indicator gives a W$_2$ coefficient of
\ppnum[3]{classification-industry-coefficient}, with 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{classification-industry-ci-low},
\ppnum[3]{classification-industry-ci-high}], and a partial $R^2$ of
\ppnum[3]{classification-industry-partial-r2} relative to the combined
firm-effects, sector, and industry baseline.
Restricting attention to within-industry pairs yields a coefficient of
\ppnum[3]{classification-within-industry-coefficient}, with 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{classification-within-industry-ci-low},
\ppnum[3]{classification-within-industry-ci-high}].
This interval is appreciably wider because the estimate uses only
\ppnum[0]{classification-within-industry-n-dyads} pairs among
\ppnum[0]{classification-within-industry-n-firms} incident firms.
Industries with only two or three firms supply no residual pair variation
after symmetric firm effects; the identifying sample comprises
\ppvalue{classification-identifying-industries} larger industries containing
\ppvalue{classification-identifying-firms} firms.

The six-rung W$_2$ ladder asks whether the association between
article-embedding distance and return distance is confined to raw sample
composition or remains after increasingly stringent adjustment for observable
and stable firm-level heterogeneity.
The sequence is increasingly demanding in the heterogeneity it absorbs,
although the final firm-effect specification changes the adjustment design
rather than simply adding another pooled control.
Specification 1 records the unadjusted W$_2$ association.
Specification 2 adds a same-sector indicator to address broad sector sorting;
because sector remains a coarse description of firm composition,
specification 3 adds the absolute difference in firms' mean log dollar
trading volume and an indicator that both return histories cover the
COVID-19 crisis window.
Specification 4 additionally declares the absolute difference and minimum
of the two firms' log article counts.
All firms cover the crisis window and have the same article count in this
sample, so the crisis indicator and both count controls are collinear with
the intercept or zero and are removed by rank selection.
Specifications 3 and 4 therefore have the same effective regressors
and estimates.
Article counts do not capture within-firm content dispersion, so
specification 5 adds the sum and absolute difference of that dispersion.
This decomposition diagnostic separates between-firm transport distance from
heterogeneity in the breadth of the two article clouds.
Because the pooled controls address only their measured dimensions,
specification 6 replaces them with symmetric firm effects that absorb stable
additive endpoint heterogeneity.
This sixth specification supplies the firm-effects benchmark; the preceding
five document the path from raw association to that design.
The classification tests in the main text extend the same firm-effects
design by including sector and industry matching directly.

\Cref{tab:dyadic-ladder} reports these six prespecified specifications.
Pooled and firm-effect $R^2$ values are not comparable because the latter
absorb endpoint margins; partial $R^2$ therefore compares the focal regressor
only with the restricted firm-effect model.

With the point design audited, the remaining inference issue is dependence
among dyads that share a firm.
For bootstrap draw $b$, the $N=\ppvalue{n-firms}$ firms receive counts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:node-bootstrap-counts}
  (w_{1b},\ldots,w_{Nb})
  \sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N;1/N,\ldots,1/N),
\end{equation}
and dyad $(i,j)$ receives frequency weight $w_{ib}w_{jb}$. The two-sided
sign-tail probability is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-bootstrap-tail}
  p_{\mathrm{boot}}
  =2\min\left\{
    \frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\1\{\widehat\beta_{W_2,b}\leq0\}}{B+1},
    \frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\1\{\widehat\beta_{W_2,b}\geq0\}}{B+1}
  \right\}\wedge1.
\end{equation}
Resampling firms rather than dyads preserves the shared-endpoint dependence:
when firm $i$ appears $w_{ib}$ times, every incident dyad inherits that
multiplicity through $w_{ib}w_{jb}$.

The add-one correction prevents zero simulated tail probabilities
\citep{phipson2010permutation}. Percentile intervals are first-order summaries
of firm-level sampling uncertainty conditional on the constructed article
and return matrices.

\subsection{Representation and article-sampling sensitivity}
\label{sec:representation-diagnostics}
\label{sec:wasserstein-sensitivity}
\label{sec:xmodel-robustness}
\label{sec:geometry-diagnostics}

Having audited the estimator and inference, we next ask whether the reported
association depends on the particular encoder, width, or distance.
\Cref{tab:w2-representation-sensitivity} holds the estimator, return matrix,
firm sample, and node-bootstrap schedule fixed while changing encoder, width,
or distance.
Rank agreement is Spearman correlation with the canonical Qwen3-Embedding-8B,
128-article W$_2$ matrix; the standardized effect is the change in return
chord distance associated with one dyadic standard deviation of the
row's metric.
Writing this effect as $s_r$ for representation $r$, every paired contrast is
$\Delta_{c,r}=s_c-s_r$; a negative value therefore means that the candidate
has the smaller standardized association.
All cell-level association intervals remain positive.
Within these comparisons, W$_1$ nearly preserves the canonical ordering,
while energy produces a materially different ordering but a similar positive
association.

The lower-cost distance comparators reuse the same deterministic article
samples and Euclidean chord ground metric, so their formulas isolate the
distance functional.
Let $A_{ij}$ denote the cross-sample average chord distance and $V_i,V_j$ the
corresponding within-firm averages.
The reported sample $V$-statistic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sample-energy-distance}
  \widehat{\mathcal E}_{ij}
  :=\mathcal E(\widehat Q_i,\widehat Q_j) = \sqrt{
    \frac{2}{N_i N_j} \sum_{n=1}^{N_i} \sum_{m=1}^{N_j}
    d_\Omega(\bm{e}_{i,n}, \bm{e}_{j,m}) - V_i - V_j
  }
\end{equation}
where $d_\Omega(\bm{x}, \bm{y}) = \norm{\bm{x} - \bm{y}}_2$ is the Euclidean
ground metric in $\R^{\ppvalue{embed-dim}}$. Each article embedding is
$L^2$-normalized to unit length, so $d_\Omega$ is the chordal metric on the
unit sphere and $V_i,V_j$ measure within-firm coverage dispersion. The
radicand is nonnegative for the biased $V$-statistic, and the resulting matrix
is symmetric with zero diagonal.

The controlled $W_1$ comparator is the equal-weight balanced assignment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-wasserstein}
  \widehat W_{1,m}(i,j)
  = \min_{\pi\in S_m}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{a=1}^{m}
  \norm{x_{ia}-x_{j,\pi(a)}}_2.
\end{equation}
It is the minimum average semantic displacement needed to align two firms'
article distributions. Unlike W$_2$, it does not give larger displacements
quadratically greater weight.

The close rank agreement between these costs motivates a hypothesis about
representation geometry.
Row normalization maps a nonzero vector $x$ to $x/\norm{x}$ on
$S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb R^d$, with $d=\ppvalue{embed-dim}$ for the canonical
encoder, and bounds chord distances between zero and two.
The Qwen training objective uses cosine similarity
\citep{qwen3_embedding_2025}; more generally, contrastive learning on
normalized representations encourages alignment of related examples and
uniformity across the sphere \citep{wang_isola_hypersphere_2020}.
We conjecture that normalization and the resulting distribution of matching
distances contribute to the observed W$_1$/W$_2$ agreement.
Over a narrow range of positive distances, squared cost is approximately
affine in distance, giving the two assignment objectives similar weights.
Normalization alone neither establishes that concentration nor fixes the
ordering of optimal assignment costs.
The measurement question is whether a representation can retain meaningful
variation in economic magnitude alongside semantic direction.
It motivates representations designed for economic measurement and a
separate assessment of the information carried by vector length.

The seven-row encoder panel targets three measurement concerns.
The capacity comparison asks whether model scale drives the association by
pairing Qwen3-Embedding-8B with its 4B variant at the same native width and
at a common 1,024-dimensional output.
The output-width comparison asks whether the estimated association depends on
the full coordinate set.
It uses Matryoshka prefixes, for which the encoder's training makes
leading-coordinate prefixes usable as nested, lower-capacity embeddings at
256 and 64 coordinates.
These prefixes are fixed before the analysis, not principal components or
selected coordinates \citep{kusupati_matryoshka_2022}.
The architecture comparison asks whether the association is specific to the
Qwen training pipeline.
It uses BGE-large-v1.5, a bidirectional encoder from a different provider and
training pipeline that changes architecture, pretraining, and provider
jointly \citep{xiao_cpack_2023}.
Paired intervals and Holm-adjusted $p$-values compare standardized
associations across representations.
W$_1$ and energy are substituted for $X_{ij}$ only in the descriptive
representation table.
Because all rows share the node schedule, endpoint effects, and omitted
indicator, their standardized effects are comparable even though one-unit
coefficients inherit different distance scales.

All supplementary slopes are positive, with pointwise node-bootstrap
intervals excluding zero (\Cref{tab:text-controls}).
The TF--IDF W$_2$ result shows that the association survives simple lexical
representations of article distributions.
Its higher fit than aggregate cosine distance reflects a joint change in
aggregation, weighting, and distance construction.

Within the masked-prediction models, both the standardized association and
partial $R^2$ decline as the masking rate rises from 30\% to 50\%.
The 30\% model has the strongest point-estimate fit among the four base
models; CLM and the 40\% model have similar fit, and the 50\% model has the weakest.
Thus the news--comovement association appears under both training objectives,
with no uniform point-estimate advantage for masked prediction.
More extensive masking does not yield a stronger measured connection to
return dependence in this sample.

not differences between objectives or masking rates.
All four base models have lower fit than lexical W$_2$ and canonical Qwen.

from the paired-contrast families above.

Training-information cutoff requires a separate design because comparisons
across off-the-shelf encoders also change architecture and training pipeline.
We use the eight published ChronoBERT checkpoints dated at each year-end from
2017 through 2024 \citep{he_chronobert_2025}.
These ModernBERT-based encoders were pretrained by masked token prediction.
Later checkpoints continue training the preceding checkpoint on newly
available text; the panel therefore combines later information with cumulative
training exposure, rather than holding token budget or compute fixed.
For each fixed checkpoint, we encode the same 128 articles per firm and average
the final-layer token states over all non-padding positions, including special
tokens, to obtain a 768-coordinate document representation.
The common inference policy retains at most 8,000 characters and 1,024 tokens
per article and uses bfloat16 autocast with float32 model parameters and
token-mean pooling.
We apply row-L2 normalization once before distance construction, without
selecting layers using returns.

The 2017 training cutoff precedes the
\ppvalue{sample-year-start}--\ppvalue{sample-year-end} article window;
2018--2022 cutoffs fall within construction, and 2023--2024 cutoffs follow it.
The last two nevertheless overlap, rather than postdate, the full 2023--2026
return-evaluation period.
Publication occurred later: the comparison concerns reported training
information, not historical deployment availability or point-in-time validity
of every other input.
All 28 later-minus-earlier contrasts use the common node-bootstrap draws, with
Holm adjustment within this vintage family.
Equivalence requires the full marginal 95\% paired interval to lie strictly
inside the symmetric inherited benchmark-calibrated margin reported in
\Cref{tab:w2-vintage-contrasts}.
This is a fixed robustness margin, not a new ChronoBERT preregistration;
the intervals are not simultaneous confidence intervals.
All annual pairs meet this inherited equivalence rule, although some paired
differences reject zero after Holm adjustment.
The estimates do not change monotonically with the cutoff year.
This combination supports bounded sensitivity under the stated margin,
not exact representation invariance or the absence of training leakage.

\subsection{Sector structure and projection validity}
\label{sec:projection-validity}
\label{sec:geometric-representation}
\label{sec:geometric-diagnostics}

Metric multidimensional scaling (MDS) constructs a descriptive two-dimensional
map by minimizing squared discrepancies between projected Euclidean distances
and the original W$_2$ distances between firms.
\Cref{fig:sector-metric-mds} displays the fitted map, whose axes have no assigned
economic meaning.
Its compression can distort both local neighbours and global distances, so
economic pair comparisons and all estimation use the original W$_2$ matrix.

    matrix for the canonical Qwen3-Embedding-8B representation.
    Both axes are unitless fitted coordinates; each labelled point is a
    priced firm, and colours identify sectors.
    Pair selection and estimation use the original W$_2$ matrix;
    \Cref{fig:metric-mds-shepard-diagnostic} reports projection fidelity.
  }
  \label{fig:sector-metric-mds}
\end{figure}

Sector structure is assessed directly in the canonical W$_2$ geometry by a
one-way permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA)
\citep{anderson_permanova_2001}.
Let $d_{ij}=W_2(Q_i,Q_j)$ for the $N$ observed firms, grouped into $K$ sectors
with sizes $n_g$.
The distance-based decomposition uses squared original distances:
\[
  SS_T=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}^2,\qquad
  SS_W=\sum_g\frac{1}{n_g}\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in g}}d_{ij}^2,
  \qquad R^2=1-\frac{SS_W}{SS_T}.
\]
The observed sector share is $R^2=\ppnum[3]{w2-permanova-r2}$, compared with
the fixed-size random-label expectation $(K-1)/(N-1)
=\ppnum[3]{w2-permanova-null-r2}$.
The pseudo-$F$ ratio divides $(SS_T-SS_W)/(K-1)$ by $SS_W/(N-K)$;
its permutation reference reassigns whole firms' labels while preserving sector
sizes and holding the distance matrix fixed.
This post-hoc diagnostic describes sector association conditional on the observed
firms and distances; inference requires exchangeability under the reference null
and does not identify a causal sector effect.

Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) provides a separate spectral diagnostic.
It double-centres the squared W$_2$ matrix, multiplies by $-1/2$, and
eigendecomposes the result.
The full spectrum distinguishes concentration in a few positive axes from
geometry that a Euclidean map cannot reproduce.
For the canonical representation, its first two positive axes retain
\ppnum[3]{pcoa-two-axis-share} of total positive inertia; this share does not
describe the metric-MDS coordinates.
For each representation, an axis's positive-inertia share divides its positive
eigenvalue by the sum of all positive eigenvalues, including axes beyond those
shown in the scree panel.
Material negative eigenvalues indicate non-Euclidean structure in the squared
distance matrix; their absolute sum divided by the sum of absolute eigenvalues
measures its negative-inertia share.
For the canonical representation this share is
\ppnum[3]{pcoa-negative-inertia-share}.
It is reported separately rather than folded into explained positive inertia.

\Cref{fig:pcoa-scree,fig:pcoa-vintage-scree} compare spectral concentration
across the seven encoder/width cells and the eight annual ChronoBERT cells,
respectively, using the same firms and each cell's 128-article W$_2$ matrix.
The return benchmark uses the eigenvalues of the same firms' unstandardized
in-sample covariance matrix.
This is PCA of returns with dates as observations and firms as variables.
Its eigenvalue shares measure explained return variance, whereas the W$_2$
curves measure positive inertia in firm-distance geometry.
Their comparison describes spectral concentration and does not measure return
variance explained by news coordinates.
\Cref{tab:pcoa-spectrum} reports the two-axis positive-inertia and negative-mass
shares for all fifteen representations.
