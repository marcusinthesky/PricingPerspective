\section{Discussion}\label{sec:limitations}

The evidence supports a compositional interpretation of economic peers.
A firm receives weight because it helps a combination of firms represent the
target's information environment.
Its relevance therefore depends on the other firms available for
reconstruction, as well as on its own resemblance to the target.
This interpretation explains why a distant firm can receive substantial
weight and why reversing the target and candidate can change the relation.
The resulting basket is a synthetic information-peer benchmark.
Across firms, these baskets provide an empirical map of a latent information
field.
Its weights describe reconstruction relevance within the observed universe;
a direct commercial link between two firms is neither required nor inferred
from a high weight.

The distinction between joint representation and pairwise proximity also
explains the comparator results.
Pairwise distances describe how separately chosen firms differ.
Reconstruction uses the candidate firms together, allowing their information
footprints to represent different parts of the target.
Equal weighting loses much of this information even when the peer set is
unchanged.
Centroid reconstruction retains most of the financial content, showing that
the joint organization of peers matters more here than the detail of the
distributional representation.
Full article distributions refine that organization by retaining within-firm
heterogeneity.
This finding gives a concrete answer to when semantic measurement is useful:
the way representations are combined into an economic object materially
affects their information about return dependence.

The information field adds a dimension of dependence to conventional factor
analysis.
Its peer weights are measured from news, and subsequent returns evaluate
their financial relevance.
Under the unrestricted firm coefficients of
\Cref{prop:factor-beta-invariance}, standard factors continue to describe
exposure to common systematic risks, while the field organizes the covariance
of residual returns.
News co-mentions contribute additional information because shared coverage
and joint representability describe different aspects of firms' information
environments.
Their partly overlapping peer maps and positive conditional held-out gains
support using both when the research question calls for these distinct
relations.

The covariance benchmark helps delimit the field's practical role.
Direct residual shrinkage has a higher held-out point score, but its paired
difference from the barycentric model is uncertain once calibration
uncertainty is included.
The comparison establishes no ranking between the two approaches at the
reported confidence level.
The field supplies an explicit, text-derived peer structure that can be
inspected and interpreted alongside its financial performance.
Peer benchmarking and spillover research can use such a structure as an input,
with causal uses requiring the additional identification described in
\Cref{sec:estimand}.
Portfolio applications require their own decision-based validation; the
appendix GMV exercise does not establish a portfolio advantage
(\ppAppendixRef{sec:app-gmv}).

The application covers continuously and heavily covered large-cap firms in a
survivor-conditioned US cross-section.
The observed text, candidate universe, and embedding model all shape the map.
Broader markets, successive information vintages, and other corpora would
test whether the same measurement captures stable peer relations across
market segments and information regimes.
These extensions would establish the reach of the latent-field
interpretation beyond the present setting.
