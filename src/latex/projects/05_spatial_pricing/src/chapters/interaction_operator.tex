\section{Measuring the Information Field}
\label{sec:interaction-operator}

Measuring the information field requires a peer basket for every firm.
The basket represents the target through a combination of other firms'
information footprints.
We collect its weights in a row of an interaction matrix $W$.
Nonnegative weights summing to one make each basket a peer average, and a zero
self-weight requires the target to be represented by other firms.

\begin{definition}[Row-stochastic, zero-diagonal interaction
  matrix]\label{def:interaction-matrix}
  An interaction matrix is any $W \in \R^{n \times n}$ with $W_{ij} \geq 0$ for
  all $i$ and $j$, $W_{ii} = 0$ for all $i$, and $\sum_j W_{ij} = 1$ for all
  $i$.
  A firm never interacts with itself, weights every other firm nonnegatively,
  and spends exactly one unit of interaction weight on the remaining firms.
\end{definition}

Each row can average a firm-level quantity in its original units, much as
benchmark weights make a benchmark return comparable with a portfolio return.
The definition specifies the form of a peer basket.
To select its members and weights, we use language-model representations of
news and find the combination that best represents the target's information
footprint within the observed universe.

A text embedding model maps each article to a fixed-dimensional vector in a
representation space, with semantically related articles lying close together.
A firm's articles therefore form an empirical characteristic law
$\widehat C_i$ in the embedding space, whose population counterpart is $C_i$.
A diversified firm occupies many positions at once, so the law records spread,
multimodality, and internal composition that one averaged embedding would
suppress.
Quadratic Wasserstein distance compares two such laws by aligning their
article clouds so as to minimize average squared displacement.
That alignment supplies article-level correspondence, but a distance alone
does not say how several candidate firms jointly represent one target.

Consider a three-firm universe with target $A$ and candidate peers $B$ and $C$.
After separately aligning the article positions of $B$ and $C$ with those of
$A$, suppose the best common convex approximation assigns coefficient $0.60$ to
the aligned positions of $B$ and $0.40$ to those of $C$ at every matched index.
In the order $(A,B,C)$, the resulting target row is
$W^\flat_{A\cdot}=(0,0.60,0.40)$.
The 60--40 weights are coordinates on aligned peer positions, not probabilities
of drawing whole articles from $B$ or $C$.

\begin{definition}[Target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction]
  \label{def:w-flat}
  Write the equally sized empirical clouds as
  $\widehat C_i=M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M\delta_{x_{im}}$.
  Let $\mathcal A_M$ be the finite set of permutations of the $M$ support
  indices.
  For every ordered pair $i\ne j$, first fix one optimal balanced assignment
  under the implementation's predetermined deterministic selection rule,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:w2-assignment}
    \pi_{ij}\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{\pi\in\mathcal A_M}
    \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M
    \lVert x_{im}-x_{j,\pi(m)}\rVert_2^2,
    \qquad y_{ijm}=x_{j,\pi_{ij}(m)}.
  \end{equation}
  Conditional on these target-specific assignments, define
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:w2-barycentre}
    w_i\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{w\in\Delta_{-i}}
    \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M
    \left\lVert x_{im}-\sum_{j\ne i}w_j y_{ijm}\right\rVert_2^2,
    \qquad
    \Delta_{-i}=\{w:w_j\geq0,\ \sum_{j\ne i}w_j=1\}.
  \end{equation}
  The output is the barycentric interaction field
  $W^\flat_{ij}=(w_i)_j$ for $j\ne i$ and $W^\flat_{ii}=0$.
\end{definition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:w2-assignment} fixes a transport alignment for each ordered
target--candidate pair, and Equation~\eqref{eq:w2-barycentre} selects the
target's simplex coordinates by minimizing reconstruction loss across all
aligned candidates jointly.
Replacing squared with unsquared norms in both stages gives the linear-cost
field $W^\flat_1$, used only to test sensitivity to the transport order.
Because the second objective is joint, a row of $W^\flat$ is not a scalar
transformation of pairwise distances.
Every other eligible firm enters the candidate set, self-links are excluded,
and the assignments are target specific.
The squared reconstruction loss, simplex normalization, equal cloud size, and
the rule that selects among tied optimal assignments are maintained design
choices.

The target remains fixed throughout this reconstruction.
An unrestricted Wasserstein barycenter holds input laws and weights fixed and
optimizes over a new center distribution $Q$, for example through
$\inf_Q\sum_{j\ne i}a_j W_2^2(C_j,Q)$
\citep{agueh_carlier_barycenters_2011}.
Here the center is the target itself, and the optimization chooses the peer
coordinates $w_i$ that best reconstruct it.

Economically, a row of $W^\flat$ is a customized information-peer basket for
one firm.
The weights are distributional spanning weights: they identify the combination
of other firms whose aligned information footprints jointly come closest to the
target's, and the objective value records how well that convex combination
represents it.
Like benchmark weights, they are nonnegative and sum to one; economically, they
measure target-specific information-peer relevance.
Because the target determines both the alignments and the reconstruction
problem, $W^\flat$ is directed even though pairwise Wasserstein distance is
symmetric.
Firm $j$ can contribute strongly to reconstructing firm $i$ while $i$
contributes little to reconstructing $j$.
The collection of these target-specific relations is the measured map of the
latent information field.

Read from left to right, transport establishes correspondence, simplex
reconstruction selects joint coordinates, and the fitted row becomes the
target's peer average.
The simplex constraint and leave-one-out policy imply, row by row,

\[
  W^\flat_{ij}\geq0,\qquad W^\flat_{ii}=0,\qquad
  \sum_{j} W^\flat_{ij}=1.
\]

The construction therefore satisfies \Cref{def:interaction-matrix} with no
separate normalization of the rows and no kernel bandwidth.

\begin{proposition}[SAR compatibility]\label{thm:wflat-sar-gate}
  Every operator satisfying \Cref{def:w-flat} is entrywise nonnegative,
  row-stochastic, and zero-diagonal. In the induced $\ell^\infty$ operator
  norm, $\lVert W^\flat\rVert\leq1$; hence $|\rho|<1$ implies
  $\lVert\rho W^\flat\rVert<1$ and is sufficient for the SAR resolvent.
\end{proposition}

The $\ell^\infty$ norm is the maximum absolute row sum, so the proposition
follows from the display above: the constructed field enters the spatial
multiplier without further normalization.
\Cref{sec:results-anatomy} displays actual rows of the estimated field.

\subsection{Comparators}\label{sec:interaction-comparators}

The comparators identify which aspects of the peer baskets carry information
about returns.
They distinguish the contribution of fitted weights, within-firm
distributions, joint reconstruction, and the source used to define a link.

The equal-active-support comparator holds fixed the active peer set selected by
$W^\flat$ and changes only the relative weights within that set.
For each row, a peer is active when $W^\flat_{ij}>10^{-8}$, a numerical zero threshold.
Writing $A_i=\{j\ne i:W^\flat_{ij}>10^{-8}\}$, the
equal-active-support matrix is

\[
  W^{\mathrm{eq}}_{ij}
  =\frac{\mathbf{1}\{j\in A_i\}}{|A_i|}.
\]

It has exactly the same support as $W^\flat$ but weights every active peer
equally, so it measures what the fitted spanning weights add to peer
selection.

The centroid comparator holds fixed the target-anchored reconstruction problem
and changes only the representation of a firm.
It collapses each firm's articles to their mean embedding
$\bar x_i=M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^M x_{im}$ and then solves the same
leave-one-out simplex problem in that averaged space, choosing for each target
the nonnegative weights summing to one that minimize
$\lVert\bar x_i-\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\bar x_j\rVert_2^2$.
It therefore retains target-specific joint reconstruction while discarding the
within-firm dispersion that the distributional representation preserves, and it
separates the two components of the barycentric construction.

The radial basis function (RBF) comparator holds fixed the empirical laws,
their pairwise quadratic
Wasserstein distances, and the candidate universe, and replaces joint target
reconstruction with a separate proximity weight for each target--candidate
pair.
For $D_{ij}=W_2(\widehat C_i,\widehat C_j)$ and bandwidth $h>0$, it sets

\[
  W^h_{ij}
  =\frac{\mathbf{1}\{j\ne i\}\exp(-D_{ij}^2/h)}
  {\sum_{k\ne i}\exp(-D_{ik}^2/h)}.
\]

The primary bandwidth is the median off-diagonal value of $D_{ij}^2$.
The comparison measures whether peer dependence follows pairwise proximity or
the joint reconstruction of a fixed target.

The persistent news co-mention comparator holds fixed the firm universe, the
2018--2022 construction window, and the row-stochastic role, and changes the
information object and link rule.
The matrix $W^{\mathrm{news}}$ links firms that are the only two tagged names in
an article and co-occur in at least two calendar years during that period;
total co-mention counts are symmetrized and row normalized.
It contrasts language-model geometry with explicit shared coverage.
Because it satisfies \Cref{def:interaction-matrix}, it can also enter the
adjustment problem as a second channel beside $W^\flat$, and
\Cref{sec:results} uses it in both roles.

The industry/sector comparator assigns equal weight to all other firms in the
target's industry. If that set is empty, it falls back to the target's sector;
if the sector is also a singleton, it assigns equal weight to the remaining
priced firms. This rule is applied once to the frozen universe and then held
fixed through the return comparison.
