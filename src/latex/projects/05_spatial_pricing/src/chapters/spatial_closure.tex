\subsection{Exposure adjustment}\label{sec:exposure-adjustment}

Peer adjustment follows an as-if reduced-form criterion that summarizes costly
operational, financing, or portfolio reconfiguration.
The criterion balances fidelity to the firm's stand-alone exposure against
alignment with peer-weighted exposures, and it accommodates several peer
definitions at once.
Joint information reconstruction and explicit news links describe different
forms of peer relevance, so we state the criterion for the barycentric field
$W^\flat$ and the news-link field $W^{\mathrm{news}}$, each satisfying
\Cref{def:interaction-matrix}; each coefficient carries its field's
superscript.
Setting $\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}=0$ leaves a single field; the single-field
results hold for
any $W$ satisfying \Cref{def:interaction-matrix}, including every comparator.
The main text writes exposures as vectors in $\mathbb R^K$; the same arguments
extend to a real separable Hilbert space as noted in the appendix.

\begin{definition}[Peer adjustment problem]\label{def:adjustment-problem}
  \label{def:two-field-adjustment}
  Let $\lambda^\flat,\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}\geq0$.
  Holding the profile $b_{-i}$ of peer exposures fixed, firm $i$ chooses
  $a\in\mathbb R^K$ to minimize
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:two-field-objective}
    \frac{1}{2}\norm{a-\xi_i}^2
    +\frac{\lambda^\flat}{2}\sum_j W^\flat_{ij}\norm{a-b_j}^2
    +\frac{\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}}{2}\sum_j W^{\mathrm{news}}_{ij}\norm{a-b_j}^2 .
  \end{equation}
  A profile $b$ is an equilibrium when every $b_i$ minimizes
  \eqref{eq:two-field-objective} at $b_{-i}$.
\end{definition}

All terms are measured in squared exposure units.
The first penalizes departure from the stand-alone exposure $\xi_i$, and the
others penalize disagreement with each peer profile; $\lambda^\flat$ and
$\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}$
are dimensionless penalty weights relative to the stand-alone term.
The quadratic form and the fixed fields are maintained inputs to the model.
Write
\[
  \begin{gathered}
    \rho^\flat=\frac{\lambda^\flat}{1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}},
    \qquad
    \rho^{\mathrm{news}}
    =\frac{\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}}{1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}},
    \\
    s=\rho^\flat+\rho^{\mathrm{news}},
    \qquad
    R=\rho^\flat W^\flat+\rho^{\mathrm{news}} W^{\mathrm{news}}.
  \end{gathered}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Peer-adjustment equilibrium]\label{prop:peer-adjustment}
  \label{thm:adjustment-equilibrium}\label{thm:spatial-closure}
  \label{thm:two-field-closure}\label{cor:mixture-nesting}
  \label{thm:sar-reduced-form}\label{prop:channel-inversion}
  \label{cor:zero-feedback-nesting}
  Let $\lambda^\flat,\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}\geq0$ and let
  $W^\flat,W^{\mathrm{news}}$ satisfy
  \Cref{def:interaction-matrix}.
  (i) A profile $b$ is an equilibrium if and only if, for every $i$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:adjustment-stationarity}
    b_i-\xi_i+\lambda^\flat\sum_j W^\flat_{ij}(b_i-b_j)
    +\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}\sum_j W^{\mathrm{news}}_{ij}(b_i-b_j)=0 .
  \end{equation}
  (ii) Every equilibrium satisfies the spatial autoregression
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:two-field-sar}
    B=\rho^\flat\,W^\flat B+\rho^{\mathrm{news}}\,W^{\mathrm{news}}B+(1-s)\,\xi .
  \end{equation}
  With a single field this is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert-sar}
    B=\rho\,WB+(1-\rho)\,\xi,
    \qquad
    \rho=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}.
  \end{equation}
  (iii) If $s>0$, then $R=sW(\theta)$ for the mixture
  $W(\theta)=\theta W^\flat+(1-\theta)W^{\mathrm{news}}$ with
  $\theta=\lambda^\flat/(\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}})$, and
  $W(\theta)$ satisfies
  \Cref{def:interaction-matrix}.
  In every case $\norm{R}=s<1$ in the $\ell^\infty$-induced operator norm,
  so the equilibrium is unique and
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert-reduced-form}
    B=(1-s)(I-R)^{-1}\xi .
  \end{equation}
  (iv) The intensities are recovered from the coefficients by
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:channel-inversion}
    \lambda^\flat=\frac{\rho^\flat}{1-s},
    \qquad
    \lambda^{\mathrm{news}}=\frac{\rho^{\mathrm{news}}}{1-s},
  \end{equation}
  which with a single field reads $\lambda=\rho/(1-\rho)$.
  (v) At zero feedback, $\lambda^\flat=\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}=0$,
  peer-adjusted exposure
  equals stand-alone exposure: $B=\xi$.
\end{proposition}

Because the rows of each field sum to one, completing the square writes
\eqref{eq:two-field-objective} as
$\tfrac12(1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}})\norm{a-m_i}^2$ plus terms
free of $a$, where
\[
  m_i=\frac{\xi_i+\lambda^\flat\sum_j W^\flat_{ij}b_j
    +\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}\sum_j
  W^{\mathrm{news}}_{ij}b_j}{1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}}.
\]
The unique minimizer is $a=m_i$, which is (i) and, after division by
$1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}}$, (ii); no differentiability
argument is required.
The mixture is entrywise nonnegative with a zero diagonal, and its rows are
convex combinations of unit row sums.
The nonnegative rows of $R$ therefore sum to $s=(\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}})/
(1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}})<1$, and the Neumann series for
$(I-R)^{-1}$ gives (iii).
Part (iv) follows from $1-s=(1+\lambda^\flat+\lambda^{\mathrm{news}})^{-1}$,
and (v) from $R=0$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:hilbert-sar} expresses spatial dependence as the equilibrium
of the adjustment problem: peer-adjusted exposure is a convex balance between
the peer average and the stand-alone exposure.
The coefficient $\rho$ is therefore a model-implied index of relative peer
adjustment.
A value $\rho=1/2$ gives both equal weight, and values approaching one place
progressively greater weight on peer alignment.
With two fields, $s$ is the total peer-adjustment weight, $\rho^\flat$ and
$\rho^{\mathrm{news}}$
divide it between the channels, and $1-s$ anchors exposure to the stand-alone
value; \eqref{eq:channel-inversion} measures each channel's coefficient against
that anchor.
Stability is automatic on this nonnegative-intensity branch.
The working return likelihood of \Cref{sec:estimation} searches the wider
interval $|\rho|<1$, a distinction \Cref{sec:estimand} keeps explicit.
The multiplier aggregates direct and iterated peer adjustment, while the factor
$1-s$ preserves the scale of the stand-alone exposures.
Part (v) recovers the exposure object of the pairwise covariance restriction in
\citet{gawronsky_continuous_2026}.

Peer adjustment also compresses cross-sectional heterogeneity in a controlled
way.
Under a nonnegative row-stochastic field, adjustment cannot increase
stationary-weighted exposure dispersion and retains at least
$\{(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)\}^2$ of it (\Cref{thm:spatial-attenuation} in
\ppAppendixRef{sec:dispersion}), so stronger peer alignment narrows exposure
differences across firms without erasing them.
\Cref{sec:identification-estimation} carries the one-field and two-field
equilibria to returns.
