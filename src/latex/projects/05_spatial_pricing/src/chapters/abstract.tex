Identifying a firm's economic peers matters for benchmarking, risk measurement,
and understanding return dependence, yet a diversified firm may have no single
natural peer.
We use language-model representations of corporate news to identify the
combination of firms that jointly represents each firm's information
environment.
Each firm is represented by a distribution of article embeddings, and
target-anchored Wasserstein barycentric reconstruction produces a directed map
of economic-peer weights.
We use this map as the interaction structure in a spatial factor model and ask
whether return dependence follows peer relations measured from news without
using returns.
We construct peer weights from 2018--2022 news for 100 large-cap US-traded
firms and evaluate their financial relevance in subsequent returns.
Relative to a matched model with the Fama--French five factors and momentum,
the information field improves held-out joint-density fit over 2023--2026,
conditional on realized factor returns.
Because unrestricted firm coefficients leave conventional factor betas
unchanged, the improvement operates through residual covariance: the field
helps organize which firms' unexpected returns move together after common
factor exposures are accounted for.
Joint reconstruction contributes substantially more than pairwise Wasserstein
proximity or equal weighting of the selected peers, while centroid
reconstruction retains most of the gain.
Explicit news co-mentions provide complementary information.
Language models thus make the interaction structure in spatial asset pricing
an object of measurement: economically interpretable peer relations can be
constructed from text before their financial relevance is assessed in returns.
