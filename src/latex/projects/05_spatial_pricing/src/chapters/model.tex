\section{Peer Adjustment and Spatial Closure}\label{sec:spatial-closure}

\subsection{Stand-alone and peer-adjusted exposures}\label{sec:model}

The measured field describes which firms jointly represent each target's
information environment.
An exposure-adjustment model gives those peer baskets a role in firms'
responses to common risks.
Begin with the familiar factor model, in which an exposure is a vector of $K$
factor loadings in $\R^K$.
A firm's \emph{stand-alone exposure} $\xi_i$ is the loading vector implied by
its own information before any peer adjustment.
Peer adjustment maps $\xi_i$ into the \emph{peer-adjusted exposure} $B_i$, and
a factor bridge links $B_i$ to returns.

The quadratic criterion below provides a reduced-form representation of the
costs of operational, financing, or portfolio reconfiguration.
Keeping $\xi_i$ and $B_i$ separate distinguishes the exposure implied by a
firm's own information from the exposure after alignment with its peers.

Embedding positions describe information content, and factor loadings measure
sensitivity to common shocks.
A transmission map connects these different quantities.
Draw $X_i\sim C_i$, let $U_i$ collect idiosyncratic transmission randomness, and
let a common measurable map $T$ convert the information draw into the exposure
space:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-exposure}
  \xi_i = T(X_i,U_i).
\end{equation}

The characteristic law is measured in embedding-space units and $\xi_i$ in
factor-exposure units.
The map $T$ and its inputs are latent.

The empirical implementation uses $\R^K$ throughout; the supporting dispersion
appendix records the corresponding Hilbert-space extension.
Let $F_t\in\R^K$ be a centered common factor innovation with covariance matrix
$\Gamma$, and let $e_t^i$ be a centered idiosyncratic return component
that is square-integrable, orthogonal to $F_t$, and uncorrelated across firms.
The return bridge specifies how peer-adjusted exposure enters centered excess
returns:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert-return}
  \widetilde r_t^i = B_i^{\prime}F_t+e_t^i.
\end{equation}

Here $B_i$ has loading units, $F_t$ has factor-innovation units, and their inner
product has return units.
The equilibrium below is solved pointwise for each realization of $\xi$, so $B$
is random whenever $\xi$ is, even when $W$ and the adjustment weight are fixed.

The adjustment problem below balances departure from stand-alone exposure
against misalignment with information peers.
Its equilibrium has spatial coefficient
\[
  \rho=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},
\]
where $\lambda$ is the relative penalty on peer misalignment.
\Cref{fig:spatial-exposure-arc} locates this model relation within the empirical
sequence from text to peer weights and return evaluation.
