\section{Related Literature}\label{sec:literature}

Text-based finance establishes that firms' information environments contain
financially relevant variation.
Attention measures, textual factors, embedding-based return and
stochastic-discount-factor models, and learned graphs for factor exposures
use that information in different ways
\citep{ben_rephael_information_consumption_2019,cong_textual_factors_2024,
wang2025newsnet,son2022graph,uddin2024network}.
Our question concerns the relations among firms that can be measured from
these representations.
We construct a basket of information peers for each target, then evaluate
whether the resulting cross-sectional structure organizes return dependence.
The distinction is between extracting information about an individual firm
and measuring how several firms jointly represent its information environment.

News networks provide a direct informational definition of a peer.
Economic linkages inferred from news predict returns, and sentence-level
co-mentions trace contagion and aggregate risk
\citep{scherbina_economic_2013,schwenkler_network_2020}.
\citet{ge_news-implied_2023} place a co-mention matrix inside a spatial factor
model, and \citet{ge_network_guided_covariance_2026} study whether auxiliary
network information improves covariance estimation.
These approaches begin with an observed or count-weighted graph.
Our reconstruction uses the content of firms' coverage to select combinations
of peers, including firms that need not appear together in an article.
A persistent co-mention field therefore provides both a natural comparison
and a complementary source of information that can enter the model jointly.

Spatial finance supplies the framework for evaluating these peer structures.
Spatial asset-pricing models describe how local interaction modifies
factor-based pricing, conditional on an interaction matrix $W$
\citep{fernandez_spatial_2011,kou_asset_2018,ge_news-implied_2023}.
In the spatial CAPM and spatial APT of \citet{kou_asset_2018}, assets occupy
point-valued locations and inverse geographic distance supplies the weights.
The spatial multiplier accumulates direct and higher-order interactions.
Classical spatial-autoregressive inference also conditions on a known $W$;
estimating weights from the outcomes entering the spatial lag creates
mechanical reflection \citep{anselin_1988,lesage_pace_2009,kelejian_prucha_2010}.
We measure peer weights from news without fitting them to the return outcomes
used in evaluation and hold the resulting field fixed during estimation.

Linear-quadratic network games explain how individual objectives generate
aggregate propagation on a given graph.
Costly local complementarity yields best responses summarized by a network
resolvent \citep{ballester_networks_2006}.
Our adjustment model applies this logic to factor exposures.
Firms balance departures from stand-alone exposure against misalignment with
peer exposures, and the spatial coefficient records their relative weights.
The measured information field supplies the peer baskets to which this
adjustment applies.

The construction connects these finance questions to Wasserstein geometry.
A distribution of articles retains information about a firm's internal
composition; if every distribution collapses to a point mass, Wasserstein
distance reduces to the underlying point distance.
Pairwise transport provides distances and article-level correspondence.
Target-anchored reconstruction uses those correspondences to select the
combination of firms that jointly represents each target.
It borrows the geometry of the Wasserstein barycenter
\citep{agueh_carlier_barycenters_2011}, while fixing the target and estimating
peer weights instead of optimizing over a free center distribution.
The centroid and pairwise-distance comparators separate the roles of
within-firm distributions and joint reconstruction in the return evidence.
Recent work uses distribution-valued firm characteristics for pairwise
covariance bounds \citep{gawronsky_continuous_2026} and portfolio allocation
\citep{gawronsky_distance_mpt_2026}; the object here is the multi-firm
information field.
