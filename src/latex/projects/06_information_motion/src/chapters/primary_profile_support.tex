\subsection{Independent firm-history sampling}\label{app:history-sampling}

The same firm appears as a target and as a reference across many comparisons.
To describe how this reuse enters the moments, write them in terms of complete
firm triples.

For an unordered triple $T=\{i,j,k\}$, define symmetric kernels by averaging
its three target assignments:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete-triples}
  H^q_y(T)=\frac13\sum_{i\in T}(R_{ijy}-R_{iky})(A_{ijy}-A_{iky}),\qquad
  H^d_y(T)=\frac13\sum_{i\in T}(R_{ijy}-R_{iky})^2,
\end{equation}
where $j,k$ are the other two members; their ordering does not affect
the product.
Expanding each centered covariance into reference-pair contrasts gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triple-identity}
  (q_y,d_y)=c_N\binom N3^{-1}\sum_T(H^q_y(T),H^d_y(T)),\qquad
  c_N=\frac{N-2}{2(N-1)}.
\end{equation}
Identity~\eqref{eq:triple-identity} expresses the all-reference moments through
overlapping triples, making their shared firm histories explicit rather than
treating the triples as independent observations.

Under independent sampling of complete firm histories, the contribution of
one firm must include every triple in which it appears.
A conditional H\'ajek benchmark projects the order-three kernels onto those
histories, preserving covariance across all years.
For $U_y=\binom N3^{-1}\sum_T H_y(T)$, a finite incident-triple contribution is
$\widehat\phi_{iy}=3c_N[\binom{N-1}{2}^{-1}\sum_{T\ni i}H_y(T)-U_y]$.
Applying the ratio derivative gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-sandwich}
  \widehat\psi_i=
  \frac{\sum_y(\widehat\phi^q_{iy}-\widehat\beta\widehat\phi^d_{iy})}{\sum_y
  d_y},\qquad
  \widehat V_{\beta}=\frac{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}_i(\widehat\psi_i)}{N}.
\end{equation}
Interpreting this benchmark as population uncertainty requires conditionally
iid complete histories given a fixed common environment, fixed symmetric
square-integrable kernels, a positive limiting denominator, a nondegenerate
first projection and a consistent covariance estimator; the empirical
incident projection is not automatically an unbiased population influence
estimate.

To assess finite-sample calibration under the iid-history assumption, let
each firm draw independent $X_i,E_i\sim N(0,1)$ and an independent
Rademacher $U_i$, retaining these variables across all years and roles.
For $t=0,\ldots,3$, use the generated arrays
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inference-fixture}
  R_{ijt}=c_t X_j,\qquad
  A_{ijt}=\beta R_{ijt}+g_t(E_j+\tfrac{1}{2}E_i X_j)+tU_i,
\end{equation}
where $c=(1,2,1,2)$ and $g=(1,1,2,2)$, with self comparisons excluded.
Reference centering removes $tU_i$.
The population ratio of expected pooled moments is $\beta$, its first
influence is $0.9E_i(X_i+1/2)$, and its asymptotic variance is $1.0125/N$.
This simulation design supplies a known sampling target while preserving
cross-year and cross-role dependence.
Its firms are sampled independently rather than jointly solving the
competitive economy.

For $H_0:\beta\leq0$ against $H_1:\beta>0$, the candidate lower bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided-candidate}
  L=\widehat\beta-z_{0.95}\sqrt{\widehat V_\beta},\qquad
  z_{0.95}=\ppnum[6]{ir-inference-z},
\end{equation}
with rejection when $L>0$.
We compare 16-firm and 100-firm panels, with
\ppnum[0]{ir-inference-n100-replications} independent replications per
target at each size and \ppnum[0]{ir-inference-n100-years} years.
Within each size, the zero and
$\beta=\ppnum[1]{ir-inference-n100-alternative-target}$ targets use the
same firm draws.
The fixed replication count bounds the worst-case Bernoulli Monte Carlo
standard error at one percentage point.
Table~\ref{tab:iid-inference-audit} reports target containment, null and
alternative rejection, and the ratio of mean candidate standard error to
empirical dispersion.
Increasing the sample size brings coverage closer to the nominal 95\%
within this sampling design, although some undercoverage remains.

\subsection{Article-selection uncertainty}\label{app:article-selection}

For article selection, independent generated archives supply the repeated
samples while firms, economic histories and reported population laws remain
fixed.
We retain all sixteen histories and the common-reporting mechanism,
stationary-law control and reporting-only law at amplitude 0.005.
Each history has \ppnum[0]{ir-redraw-outer-replications} outer archives
at observed expanded coverage.
Within each firm-year, the canonical and additional article strata retain
their frozen sample sizes and monthly date probabilities.
An outer archive draws dates within each stratum and article atoms conditional
on those dates from the reported law.

Each of its \ppnum[0]{ir-redraw-inner-redraws} inner redraws resamples the
empirical joint date--atom counts separately by stratum at unchanged sizes.
The same counts determine article geometry and article-timing weights, and
each origin draw is reused in every target and reference role.

To assess whether the redraw ranges capture the reported-law coefficient,
we use the population projection for the prescribed stratum-specific date
distributions in each fixed history as the primary target.
The archive projection and the population projection conditional on its
article dates are also recorded.
Linear empirical quantiles give the 2.5th--97.5th percentile range and fifth
percentile.
We measure how often the range contains the target, how often the target
lies above the fifth percentile, and how often that percentile exceeds zero.
Table~\ref{tab:article-redraw-validation} averages these events equally
across histories; its Monte Carlo errors use independent outer archives,
not the inner redraws as separate replications.
Quantiles use defined inner estimates, matching the corporate procedure;
all attempted inner draws and unavailable outer outputs remain counted.
The stationary law has a zero target, while the reporting-only law has a
potentially nonzero reported-law target despite fixed capacities.

An exact empirical transport problem can have a nonunique optimal dual face,
so fixed-support mass perturbations may have different left and right
derivatives.
Finite directional sensitivities and the linear ratio derivative alone do
not establish smooth article influence functions; finite-sample containment
under the specified observation law is distinct from a general bootstrap
validity result.

The iid-history and nested-archive experiments concern distinct sampling
populations.
Jointly solved competitors do not become independent histories by conditioning
on common news, and the corporate archive of Nasdaq financial news, collected
in December
2022--January 2023 with operating reports and market commentary from multiple
publishers, has no established iid-history sampling design.
Its article redraws condition on the retained firms, returns and years.
The application therefore reports article-selection sensitivity rather than
a corporate population confidence bound or $p$-value.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the pooled projection}\label{app:projection-sensitivity}

The complete-triple representation gives all-role deletion moments by
removing every triple incident to the deleted firm and using the remaining
sample's normalization.
Consequently a firm is removed everywhere it appears, rather than only as a
target or as one financial reference.

When annual ratios differ, changing their weights changes the pooled
summary.
A fixed-weight annual GMM criterion makes that dependence explicit alongside
the moment ratio in \eqref{eq:directional-ratio}.
For $q=(q_y)_y$, $d=(d_y)_y$ and a fixed symmetric positive-definite weight
$W$, minimizing $(q-\beta d)^\top W(q-\beta d)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annual-gmm}
  \beta_W=\frac{d^\top Wq}{d^\top Wd},\qquad
  d\beta_W=\frac{d^\top W\,dq+(q-2\beta_W d)^\top W\,dd}{d^\top Wd}.
\end{equation}
Both annual numerators and denominators vary; the term involving $dd$
retains the common-slope misspecification residual.
At $W=I$, annual ratios receive weights proportional to $d_y^2$.
The original pool weights them by $d_y$.
For the primary cell, the fixed-identity slope is \ppnum[6]{ir-observation-gmm-beta} and
2020 receives \ppnum[1]{ir-observation-gmm-2020-weight-pct}\% effective weight rather
than \ppnum[1]{ir-primary-2020-weight-pct}\%.
This is a different annual compromise, not an efficiency improvement to an
exactly identified ratio.
No estimated optimal weighting or chi-square reference for a
specification test is used.

The deletion checks remove observed firms, but measurement errors can also
alter the pooled alignment.
A deterministic error frontier bounds such changes under hypothetical
error limits.
Write observed centered arrays as $R=R^{\mathrm{core}}+v$ and
$A=A^{\mathrm{core}}+e$.
Then the core alignment is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-frontier}
  \langle R-v,A-e\rangle
  =\langle R,A\rangle-\langle R,e\rangle-\langle v,A\rangle+\langle v,e\rangle.
\end{equation}
If the unobserved errors satisfy $\|v\|\leq\lambda_R\|R\|$ and
$\|e\|\leq\lambda_A\|A\|$, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the absolute departure by
$\|R\|\|A\|(\lambda_R+\lambda_A+\lambda_R\lambda_A)$.
The primary normalized alignment is \ppnum[5]{ir-normalized-alignment}; equal
error budgets below \ppnum[2]{ir-observation-equal-budget-pct}\% preserve a positive
finite core alignment under this conservative bound.
Those percentages are hypothetical relative RMS error limits, not estimated
reporting errors or confidence levels.
Crossing the frontier removes the sufficient guarantee without constructing a
sign reversal.
