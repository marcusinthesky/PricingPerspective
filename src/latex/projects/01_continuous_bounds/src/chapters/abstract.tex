Firm characteristics are commonly represented as fixed vectors, even though
evidence about firms' operations arrives as heterogeneous collections of
articles and reports.
We represent firms by distributions and derive restrictions on the systematic
covariance their factor exposures can support.
Quadratic Wasserstein distance measures the minimum displacement needed to
align two distributions; in exposure space, it determines their sharp
covariance ceiling.
A common characteristic-to-exposure benchmark with bounded distortion and
firm-specific departures transfers this restriction to characteristic space.
The framework makes explicit how transmission uncertainty widens the range
of feasible covariance ceilings.
We construct firm distributions from language-model embeddings of financial
news, disclosures, and analyst reports for Nasdaq firms over
\ppvalue{sample-year-start}--\ppvalue{sample-year-end}.
Greater article-embedding distance is associated with weaker return
co-movement after symmetric firm effects: the return-distance coefficient is
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-coef}, with 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-ci-low}, \ppnum[3]{confound-w2-ci-high}].
The association remains positive after sector, industry, and 10-K
product-market controls, and retains its sign in a later return window.
A sensitivity grid shows how quickly the covariance restriction loses
informativeness as transmission tolerances widen.
Together, the framework and evidence support screening firm pairs for shared
exposures and assessing covariance under explicit economic scenarios.
