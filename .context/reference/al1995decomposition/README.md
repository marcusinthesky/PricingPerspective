# Decomposition and characterization of risk with a continuum of random variables

## Summary

The article characterizes risk in a static economy with a continuum of random
variables, separating pervasive factor risk from idiosyncratic risk that averages
out across the continuum.

## Key findings

### F1 — Aggregate and idiosyncratic risk separate on an asset continuum

- Claim: A finite factor structure captures aggregate risk while idiosyncratic
  risk diversifies away across the continuum.
- Evidence: The published article is *Econometrica* 63(5):1195–1224, DOI
  10.2307/2171727.
- Scope: The continuum is over assets under an exact factor structure and uses
  standard countably additive measure theory.

### F2 — The Main Theorem has a published measurability correction

- Claim: The 1999 Corrigendum adds a measurability regularity condition to the
  Main Theorem.
- Evidence: The correction is *Econometrica* 67(4):919–920, DOI
  10.1111/1468-0262.00060.
- Scope: Both documents are held locally — the 1995 article at `raw/paper.pdf`
  and the corrigendum at `raw/corrigendum-1999.pdf`. Any use of the Main
  Theorem must carry the added regularity condition.

## Assessment

Use the article to distinguish continuum-over-assets factor decomposition from
a metric-distance bound on covariance. Cite the 1995 result only together with
the 1999 measurability condition.

## Detailed analysis

The article approximates the continuum by finite collections of random
variables to make the factor decomposition well defined. Its measure-theoretic
setting differs from the Loeb-space construction associated with
`khan2001asymptotic`, and its continuum is over assets rather than factors.
