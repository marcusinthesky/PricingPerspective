3.1 Radii propagate through the embedding

Theorem 23 Inner-product perturbation
#

Uses

  • Definition 11
  • Lemma 3

For mean embeddings perturbed by at most \(\varepsilon _i\) and \(\varepsilon _j\), the inner product moves by at most \(\varepsilon _i\lVert \mu _j\rVert +\varepsilon _j\lVert \mu _i\rVert +\varepsilon _i\varepsilon _j\).

Proof ▼

Expand the perturbed inner product and bound each cross term.

Theorem 24 Radii add on the metric
#

Uses

  • Definition 11

Under the same radii, \(\lvert \operatorname {dist}(\mu _i',\mu _j')-\operatorname {dist}(\mu _i,\mu _j)\rvert \le \varepsilon _i+\varepsilon _j\). Additivity on the metric, rather than on the squared quantity, is the same structural fact that makes the metric-level bracket of ??cor:random-w2-metric sharper than its squared counterpart.

Proof ▼

Two applications of the triangle inequality.

Theorem 25 Asymmetric squared bracket
#

Uses

  • Theorem 24
  • Theorem 24

Squaring the metric bracket gives an asymmetric interval, with the lower end truncated at zero. The asymmetry is not cosmetic: it is why the squared-cost formalization cannot simply inherit the metric statement.

Proof ▼

Square both ends of ??thm:dist-bracket and truncate.

Theorem 26 Composed covariance bracket
#

Uses

  • Definition 11
  • Theorem 25
  • Theorem 25
  • Theorem 23
  • Lemma 3

Under bi-Lipschitz constants stated in the true, unobserved distance and per-asset embedding radii, the covariance inner product is bracketed by polarization expressions evaluated at the radius-inflated and radius-deflated measured distance. The hypothesis that the bi-Lipschitz relation holds in the unobserved distance is the load-bearing one and is not testable from the measured distances.

Proof ▼

Compose ??thm:energy-bracket with the bi-Lipschitz relation and apply polarization at both ends.
