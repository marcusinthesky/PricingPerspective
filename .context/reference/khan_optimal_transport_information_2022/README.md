# When Optimal Transport Meets Information Geometry

## Summary

Khan and Zhang survey how optimal transport and information geometry provide
distinct but connected geometric structures on families of probability laws.

## Key findings

### F1 — Optimal transport and information geometry compare probability laws differently

- Claim: Both frameworks compare probability laws, but Wasserstein geometry
  does so through optimal transport while classical information geometry uses
  divergence functions.
- Evidence: `paper.md:623-632` states the comparison; `paper.md:695-708`
  contrasts divergence-based vertical displacement with transport through the
  sample space.
- Scope: The survey compares mathematical frameworks and does not establish an
  economic transmission map.

### F2 — Transport information geometry pulls Wasserstein structure onto statistical manifolds

- Claim: The transport-information-geometry strand studies statistical
  manifolds using the Wasserstein metric induced by the metric of the
  underlying space.
- Evidence: `paper.md:1042-1069` describes the pullback construction, names the
  strand, and records natural-gradient applications.
- Scope: Those applications concern statistical geometry and inference, not
  systematic return covariance.

## Assessment

Use the survey to define and delimit the Wasserstein-information-geometry
lineage. The paper's covariance envelope is a separate economic result and
must be supported by its maintained transmission assumptions and proofs.
