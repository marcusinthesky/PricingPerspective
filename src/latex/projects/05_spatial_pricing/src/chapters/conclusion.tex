\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Our results support a compositional view of a firm's economic peers.
A firm's relevant peer need not be the single firm most similar to it.
It can instead be a weighted combination of firms whose information
environments jointly represent different parts of its activities.
Language-model representations make those activities measurable from corporate
news, and target-anchored optimal transport converts them into firm-specific
peer baskets.
Across firms, those baskets form a directed information field.

The broader contribution is to make a familiar object in spatial finance, the
interaction structure linking firms, an object of measurement.
Spatial asset-pricing models require a matrix $W$ describing which firms
interact and with what weight.
Rather than specifying that matrix from geography, industry membership, or
contemporaneous returns, we measure it from an earlier information environment
and then ask whether subsequent return dependence follows the resulting
structure.
In this sense, language models serve as a measurement instrument rather than a
return-prediction device: they provide a representation from which an
economically interpretable financial object can be constructed and
independently evaluated.

The return evidence gives that measured object financial content.
The field constructed from 2018--2022 news improves held-out joint-density fit
over 2023--2026 after conditioning on the Fama--French five factors and
momentum.
Because unrestricted firm coefficients leave conventional factor betas
unchanged, the gain lies in residual covariance: information about which firms'
unexpected returns move together after common factor exposures are accounted
for.
The comparator evidence further identifies what matters in the measurement.
Target-specific joint reconstruction contains substantially more information
than pairwise proximity, fitted peer weights matter beyond peer selection, and
full article distributions add a smaller increment beyond centroid
reconstruction.
Explicit news co-mentions contribute complementary information, indicating
that joint representability and direct shared coverage capture distinct
dimensions of economic relatedness.

This distinction matters wherever economic information is richer than the
available history of return co-movement.
Firms accumulate observable information about products, customers, suppliers,
technologies, and risks that need not conform to a single industry
classification and can precede a long trading record.
Our evidence does not establish causal transmission or portfolio gains, nor
does it test newly listed firms directly.
It establishes the prior measurement result: a peer structure recovered from
earlier text contains information about subsequent financial dependence.

More generally, the value of AI for empirical finance need not lie only in
improving prediction.
It can lie in making previously latent economic objects measurable and placing
them back inside interpretable financial models.
Here, language models provide the representation, optimal transport organizes
it into economic peers, and spatial asset pricing supplies the financial
discipline with which that structure is evaluated.
The result is a measurable map of the information environment in which firms
are embedded, and a framework for asking how that environment is reflected in
capital markets.
