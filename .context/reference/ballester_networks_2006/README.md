# Who's Who in Networks

## Summary

The authors study finite-population noncooperative games with linear-quadratic payoffs and
local complementarities. Equilibrium actions are proportional to Bonacich centrality, linking
network position to Nash equilibrium and policy targeting.

## Key findings

### F1 — Linear-quadratic local interaction generates a network-resolvent equilibrium

- Claim: under the paper's parameter restriction, the network game has a unique Nash
  equilibrium proportional to Bonacich centrality.
- Evidence: the resolvent definition appears at `paper.md:207-245`; Theorem 1 and its
  equilibrium discussion appear at `paper.md:271-304`.
- Scope: the theorem is stated for the paper's linear-quadratic game and parameter region;
  it is not an asset-pricing identification result.

### F2 — The equilibrium link is a best-response result, not a network-construction result

- Claim: the network is an input to the game, and the resolvent summarizes equilibrium
  amplification over that network.
- Evidence: the model-to-centrality development appears at `paper.md:207-290`.
- Scope: the paper does not derive a network from distribution-valued text or transport
  geometry.

## Assessment

The paper supplies the established linear-quadratic network-game lineage for Paper 5's
quadratic adjustment closure. Paper 5 should claim novelty for its distribution-valued
primitive, transport-derived operator, and dispersion certificate, not for the generic
best-response-to-resolvent step.

## Detailed analysis

Ballester et al. provide a clean citation for the economic-game antecedent without forcing
Paper 5 into a broad network-games literature review. One sentence in the related-literature
section is sufficient to establish priority and sharpen the paper's actual contribution.
