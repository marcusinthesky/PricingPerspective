\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Representing firms by distributions preserves the heterogeneity of their
risk-relevant information and permits covariance restrictions to be stated
in terms of distances between probability laws.
For given exposure laws, quadratic Wasserstein geometry determines sharp
covariance endpoints.
A common transmission benchmark with bounded firm-specific departures
connects characteristic distance to the covariance ceiling.
Holding exposure magnitudes fixed, economic characteristics that remain
separated under admissible transmission leave less scope for shared
systematic risk.

The Nasdaq application gives this framework an observable counterpart.
A one-standard-deviation increase in article-embedding distance corresponds
to roughly eight fewer correlation points after symmetric firm effects.
The association persists across sector and industry controls, within
industries, after conditioning on 10-K product-market similarity, and in a
later return window.
These comparisons show that news distributions contain information about
co-movement beyond the recorded classifications and product-market measure.

The sensitivity grid identifies where the theoretical restriction is useful.
Tight transmission scenarios discriminate among covariance comparisons;
modest increases in distortion or firm-specific slack leave many comparisons
unresolved.
The practical value therefore lies in combining information about a firm's
reported activities with an explicit account of its exposure to common shocks.
Article distance directs attention to pairs worth examining, while the
envelope makes the role of contracts, hedging, financing, and transmission
uncertainty explicit in assessing their potential systematic covariance.
