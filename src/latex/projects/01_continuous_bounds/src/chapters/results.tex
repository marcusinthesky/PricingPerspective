\section{Empirical results and discussion}\label{sec:results}

Article distance is positively associated with return distance across both
return windows and after classification and product-market controls.
We present that evidence first, examine its stability across representations,
and then assess the informativeness of the covariance restriction.

\subsection{Article distance and return co-movement}
\label{sec:post-window-dyadic}

Greater article-embedding distance is associated with weaker return
co-movement after symmetric firm effects.
Across representative dyads in the interquartile range, a pair one standard
deviation farther apart in news content has a fitted return correlation
roughly eight points lower.
Return-chord distance maps deterministically to the familiar correlation
scale through \eqref{eq:chord-distance}, because $\hat\rho=1-\hat D^2/2$.
At the median dyad, a chord distance of \ppnum[3]{chord-median} corresponds
to a return correlation of \ppnum[3]{chord-rho-median}.
A one-standard-deviation increase in W$_2$ moves the implied correlation to
\ppnum[3]{chord-rho-after-median}, a change of
\ppnum[3]{chord-delta-rho-median}.
The corresponding changes are \ppnum[3]{chord-delta-rho-q25} at the
lower-quartile dyad and \ppnum[3]{chord-delta-rho-q75} at the
upper-quartile dyad.

\Cref{tab:w2-primary-results} reports the same symmetric-firm-effect
estimator for both return windows.
In the shared 2018--2022 window, the focal coefficient is
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-coef} return-chord units per unit of W$_2$, with
standard error \ppnum[3]{confound-w2-se} and 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-ci-low}, \ppnum[3]{confound-w2-ci-high}].
A one-standard-deviation increase in W$_2$ corresponds to
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-effect-one-sd} return-chord units, and W$_2$ accounts
for \ppnum[3]{confound-partial-r2} of residual variation relative to
symmetric firm effects.
The positive estimate is consistent with the directional expectation in
\Cref{hyp:monotonicity}.

The positive association persists in the later return window.
The exercise fixes the 2018--2022 article-embedding geometry before
recomputing return chord distances and firm effects for
\ppvalue{oos-return-panel-date-start} through
\ppvalue{oos-return-panel-date-end}.
The coefficient rises to \ppnum[3]{oos-dyadic-w2-coef}, with 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{oos-dyadic-w2-ci-low}, \ppnum[3]{oos-dyadic-w2-ci-high}], and the
standardized effect is \ppnum[3]{oos-dyadic-w2-effect-one-sd}.
Thus the same article geometry is associated with co-movement in both
return windows.

Classification controls next assess how much of it remains after accounting
for familiar measures of pair-level economic similarity.

\subsection{Sector, industry, and product-market
comparisons}\label{sec:classification-controls}

\Cref{tab:classification-controls} adds sector matching directly to the
primary symmetric-firm-effects design.
The coefficient remains positive under this combined adjustment.
The W$_2$ coefficient is \ppnum[3]{classification-sector-coefficient}, with
bootstrap standard error \ppnum[3]{classification-sector-se} and 95\%
interval [\ppnum[3]{classification-sector-ci-low},
\ppnum[3]{classification-sector-ci-high}].
For a one-standard-deviation increase in distance, the fitted chord contrast
is \ppnum[3]{classification-sector-effect-one-sd}, corresponding to a change
of \ppnum[3]{classification-sector-delta-correlation} in implied correlation
at the median pair.
Distance explains \ppnum[3]{classification-sector-partial-r2} of the
variation remaining after both firm effects and sector matching.

Adding same-industry matching, or restricting the fit to within-industry
pairs, leaves the coefficient positive with a 95\% interval excluding zero;
\Cref{sec:empirical-robustness} discusses these finer fits.

Industry labels are coarse relative to product-market similarity measured
from firms' own disclosures.
The last two rows of \Cref{tab:classification-controls} therefore restrict the
panel to the \ppnum[0]{etnic-n-firms} firms covered by the Hoberg--Phillips
ETNIC network and add its 10-K business-description similarity, averaged over
fiscal years \ppvalue{etnic-year-start}--\ppvalue{etnic-year-end}
\citep{hoberg2025scope}.
News W$_2$ and 10-K business-description distance have correlation
\ppnum[3]{etnic-matrix-correlation-w2}
(permutation $p\le\ppnum[4]{etnic-matrix-correlation-pvalue-max}$ under
  \ppnum[0]{etnic-matrix-correlation-permutations} joint row-and-column
firm relabelings).
After removing sector matching from both matrices, their descriptive
correlation is \ppnum[3]{etnic-matrix-correlation-w2-partial-sector}.
This agreement provides convergent evidence across news and disclosure text.
Both sources reflect reporting choices, and journalists also draw on company
disclosures.
Adding ETNIC similarity to firm effects and sector matching lowers the W$_2$
coefficient from \ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-roster-coefficient} to
\ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-coefficient}, with 95\% interval
[\ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-ci-low},
\ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-ci-high}].
Distance explains \ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-partial-r2} of the variation
remaining after firm effects, sector matching, and ETNIC similarity; ETNIC
similarity explains \ppnum[3]{classification-etnic-control-partial-r2} after
the other terms.
Both rows use the same firms and resampling draws, so the change reflects the
control rather than the sample.
The remaining association is consistent with additional economic content in
news, imperfect measurement of product similarity, and shared reporting
patterns.
The regression establishes its incremental contribution conditional on the
recorded ETNIC measure.

\subsection{Stability across representations}
\label{sec:representation-sensitivity}

Consistent with \citet{box2018qualitative}, lexical similarity is associated
with return comovement.
This association also appears when firms are represented as distributions
of TF--IDF article vectors: greater W$_2$ distance is associated with greater
return distance after symmetric firm effects, with a 95\% node-bootstrap
interval excluding zero and a partial $R^2$ of
\ppnum[3]{text-controls-tfidf-document-partial-r2}
(\Cref{tab:text-controls}).
These results support the empirical usefulness of distribution-valued firm
representations and Wasserstein comparison beyond the headline
language-model representation.

The positive distance association persists across encoder families,
embedding widths, alternative distance summaries, and reported
training-information vintages.
Moderate compression preserves the association, whereas the more severe
compression steps attenuate it, showing where the loss of semantic
coordinates weakens the measured relation.
The Qwen capacity variants and the BGE representation retain positive
associations, and their paired differences from the relevant references are
not distinguishable after adjustment.
W$_1$ nearly preserves the canonical dyad ordering, whereas energy retains a
positive association under a materially different ordering.
The close W$_1$/W$_2$ agreement may partly reflect the geometry imposed by
unit normalization.
This maps embeddings to the unit sphere, retaining direction while removing
vector length, as in many contrastive representation systems
\citep{wang_isola_hypersphere_2020}.
If the distances relevant to article matching occupy a narrow range,
proportional and quadratic costs can produce similar orderings.
This suggests a measurement question: whether representations designed to
preserve economically meaningful variation in both direction and magnitude
would distinguish the two costs more sharply.
\Cref{sec:representation-diagnostics} develops this interpretation as a
hypothesis about the representation.

The ChronoBERT annual comparisons meet the inherited benchmark-calibrated
equivalence rule for every pair, but some differences are distinguishable
from zero after vintage-family Holm adjustment
(\Cref{tab:w2-vintage-contrasts}).
Later training cutoffs neither monotonically strengthen nor monotonically
weaken the standardized association.
Thus the paired differences are bounded under this robustness margin,
not exactly zero; cumulative training also prevents a causal interpretation
as the effect of later information alone.
\Cref{tab:w2-representation-sensitivity} reports the annual cells, with
construction details and article-sampling checks in
\Cref{sec:representation-diagnostics}.

\subsection{Covariance sensitivity}\label{sec:fixed-w2-test}

The association establishes the empirical relevance of article distance.
The sensitivity grid addresses the next question: how tightly must economic
transmission be specified for that distance to restrict covariance informatively?
\Cref{tab:random-exposure-w2} applies the interval in
\Cref{eq:random-transport-excess-bracket} across nine transmission scenarios.
The zero-slack isometric cell $(L,\tau)=(1,0)$ gives nonnegative intervals for
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-l1-tau0-coverage} of observed dyads and violations for
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-l1-tau0-violation}, resolving every interval's sign.
With $(L,\tau)=(1,0.05)$, the unresolved share rises to
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-l1-tau0p05-unresolved}; at $(1,0.10)$ it reaches
\ppnum[3]{confound-w2-l1-tau0p1-unresolved}.
Even modest firm-specific departures therefore widen the interval enough to
leave most comparisons unresolved.

For an investment manager assessing a prospective holding, the workflow
begins by comparing its distribution of reported activities with those of
existing holdings, including firms outside its sector.
The analyst then reviews the underlying articles for shared customers,
suppliers, and contracts, and examines how pricing power, hedging, and
financing transmit those risks to equity returns.
Those economic judgments specify the transmission scenario and exposure
magnitudes used to assess candidate covariances against the ceiling.
The sensitivity grid shows how precise these inputs must be for that
assessment to discriminate among candidate covariances.
The neighbour paths in \Cref{sec:exploratory-geometry-outcomes} illustrate
the range of return behaviour that the economic review must explain.
