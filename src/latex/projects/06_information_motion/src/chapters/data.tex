\subsection{Empirical Design and Data}\label{sec:data}

To examine whether the association that the simulation recovers only imperfectly
also appears in corporate news, we apply the estimator of
Section~\ref{sec:adjustment-design} to \ppnum[0]{ir-firms}
Nasdaq-listed firms over five article years from 2018 through 2022, giving four
transitions ending in 2019--2022.

We measure semantic similarity between articles by embedding them with
Qwen3-Embedding-4B \citep{qwen3_embedding_2025}, a four-billion-parameter
transformer developed for information retrieval.
Because the model postdates the sample, these vectors give a retrospective
representation of the articles.
We assess the role of representation by comparing its native
\ppnum[0]{ir-native-dimension}-dimensional vectors with a compressed
64-dimensional representation.
As the model is trained with Matryoshka representation learning
\citep{kusupati_matryoshka_2022}, we obtain the compressed vectors from the first
64 coordinates of each native vector, renormalized to unit length.

We also compare two article samples within each representation to measure
sensitivity to the amount and balance of coverage.
While the expanded sample contains \ppnum[0]{ir-expanded-articles} existing
article embeddings, the matched sample restricts these to the first 128 per firm
in URL-hash order after the corpus date and body-length filters, giving
\ppnum[0]{ir-matched-articles} articles.
Taking the native expanded specification as primary, we treat the other three as
diagnostic comparisons, none selected by its financial coefficient.
All retain the same roster, origin references and annual financial windows,
but changing the article sample changes both geometry and the dates entering
\eqref{eq:dated-financial}.

For \ppnum[0]{ir-redraws} paired conditional article redraws, each firm-year
cloud is redrawn once within its canonical and additional-article strata and
reused in every target and reference role.
Origin draws remain shared across all transitions and representations, and
the return weights are recomputed from the sampled article dates.
The reported 2.5th, 5th and 97.5th percentiles describe article-selection
sensitivity conditional on the observed articles, firms, financial histories
and four annual environments.
Shared stories, common shocks and reused references make the
\ppnum[0]{ir-coordinates} directional coordinates dependent.
