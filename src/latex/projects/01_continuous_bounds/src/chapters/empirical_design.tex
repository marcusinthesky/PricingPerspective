\section{Data and empirical design}\label{sec:empirical-design}

The application represents firms by distributions of financial articles and
relates their separation to return co-movement.
We first construct the sample and its article geometry, then estimate the
distance association with firm effects and classification controls.
A sensitivity grid applies the covariance restriction across transmission scenarios.
\Cref{sec:data-provenance} documents retrieval, filtering, and data provenance.

\subsection{Sample and return construction}\label{sec:sample-and-window}

Firm-level distribution comparisons require sufficient text and common
coverage across the fixed window to construct a common-size empirical measure
for every firm, which motivates a coverage-conditioned sample rather than a
broad but uneven news universe.
The news universe is a prespecified, Nasdaq-100-based frame of
\ppvalue{n-firms-news} Nasdaq-listed firms.
Each retained firm had at least \ppvalue{annual-raw-article-floor} raw articles
in every calendar year from
\ppvalue{sample-year-start} through \ppvalue{sample-year-end} in Nasdaq's
public per-symbol news archive.
Within the fixed frame, articles satisfying the date-window and
minimum-body-length filters are ordered by URL hash, and up to
\ppvalue{n-samples-cap} are retained over the pooled window.
This coverage rule gives each firm a sufficiently supported empirical
distribution on a common observation window.
\Cref{tab:sample-descriptives-sectors} reports the sector composition,
per-firm article counts, return volatilities, and within- versus cross-sector
W$_2$ distances.
\Cref{tab:sample-flow} records the resulting flow from source records to
estimation dyads; the full retrieval and provenance protocol is in
\Cref{sec:data-provenance}.

\input{generated/sample_descriptives}

Two features of that composition motivate later design choices.
First, mean daily return volatility differs markedly across sectors, whereas
the primary return chord distance is constructed from correlations and is
therefore invariant to firm-specific return scale.
Symmetric firm effects instead absorb stable endpoint heterogeneity.
Second, mean within-sector W$_2$ distance is smaller than the corresponding
cross-sector distance in every sector holding more than one firm, so
article-embedding distance encodes sector information before any return is used.
The sector diagnostic in \Cref{sec:geometric-representation} quantifies that
pattern, and both the pooled ladder and the classification-adjusted
firm-effects regressions probe it.

To measure co-movement independently of firm-specific return scale, the
outcome is constructed from correlations of daily simple returns.
Daily simple returns are Yahoo Finance adjusted-close ratios minus one.
Return-dependent tests use the exact complete-case matrix of
\ppvalue{return-panel-n-observations} dates shared by
\ppvalue{return-panel-n-tickers} priced firms, spanning
\ppvalue{return-panel-date-start} through \ppvalue{return-panel-date-end}.
Nullable observations are removed before matrix construction, with no
interpolation or zero filling.
The primary analysis relates news and returns over the common
\ppvalue{sample-year-start}--\ppvalue{sample-year-end} window.
Return alignment and sector-metadata details are documented in
\Cref{sec:data-provenance}.

\subsection{Article-embedding representation and distance}
\label{sec:distance-construction}\label{sec:article-proxy-model}

The empirical bridge requires a common representation of heterogeneous
articles while retaining each firm as a distribution rather than collapsing
its text to a single point.
Embeddings provide that measurement technology by mapping documents into a
shared numerical space in which semantic proximity can be compared.
An embedding model is a neural network that maps a text document to a
fixed-length numerical vector---its \emph{embedding}.
Documents discussing similar topics are placed nearby in the resulting
high-dimensional space, whereas documents on unrelated subjects are placed
farther apart.
A firm's collection of vectors can therefore be treated as an empirical
distribution in semantic space.

A bi-encoder processes each document independently into a reusable vector,
giving all firm distributions a common geometry for Wasserstein comparison.

Let $C_i^\ast$ denote firm $i$'s latent economic characteristic law,
corresponding to $C_i$ in the theory, and let $Q_i$ be the law of its reported
content after encoding by $\phi$.
With $N_i$ observed articles, the empirical distribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-embedding-measure}
  \widehat Q_i
  :=\frac{1}{N_i}\sum_{d=1}^{N_i}\delta_{\phi(d)}.
\end{equation}
Distances between $\widehat Q_i$ supply the empirical measure of firm separation.
Their economic interpretation depends on how reporting and encoding preserve
information about $C_i^\ast$.

Financial news is an economically selected information product.
Investors seek information that helps them assess expected cash flows,
discount rates, and shared risks.
Information producers choose which firms and developments to investigate
under reporting costs, demand for coverage, and reputational constraints.
A story about fuel costs may therefore be useful to investors in several
airlines, while the value of investigating one firm's hedge contracts is
more concentrated.
When producing information is costly but distributing it to additional users
is cheap, widely demanded information can be supplied at a lower cost per
user.
Veldkamp's information-market model shows how these incentives encourage
shared information acquisition and can themselves generate asset-price
comovement \citep{veldkamp_information_markets_2006}.
This provides a reason for economic content to appear repeatedly across
firms' news, while also identifying a source of comovement beyond common
operating exposures.

Reports can supply overlapping evidence about a common economic state.
News of demand for one premium smartphone, for example, informs an assessment
of category demand as well as the producer's competitive position.
The firms' economic roles and investors' prior expectations determine how
that information translates into valuation.

News producers also select stories for audience appeal and professional
reasons.
Survey evidence indicates that financial journalists value accuracy,
timeliness, and depth, but also select provocative companies and personalities
for coverage \citep{call_meet_press_2022}.
Reputation incentives can coexist with persistent reporting bias
\citep{gentzkow_media_bias_2006}.
Article frequencies therefore reflect both economic activity and the
incentives to report it.

A stylized selection mechanism clarifies the measurement requirement.
Before text generation and encoding, let $s_i(x)\ge0$ be the measurable
reporting intensity of economic characteristic $x$, with
$0<\int s_i\,dC_i^\ast<\infty$.
The distribution of characteristics selected for reporting is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:news-selection}
  \widetilde C_i^\ast(dx)
  :=\frac{s_i(x)C_i^\ast(dx)}{\int s_i(u)C_i^\ast(du)}.
\end{equation}
If $s_i(x)=a_i s(x)$ for a positive firm-specific multiplier $a_i$,
normalization removes that multiplier but retains the common topic weighting
$s(x)$.
Consequently, article frequencies represent reported economic salience rather
than revenue shares or economic event probabilities.
Comparable reporting incentives motivate cross-firm measurement, but even a
common topic weighting need not preserve economic distances.
Through text generation and encoding, the coverage law $Q_i$ is generated
from the selected law $\widetilde C_i^\ast$ rather than from $C_i^\ast$ itself.
The empirical interpretation therefore requires the following premise about
the distortions introduced by selection and representation, separately from
the transmission bounds.

\begin{assumption}[Measurement Premise: Article-Embedding
  Proxy]\label{ass:article-proxy}
  After symmetric firm margins and the declared controls, distortions from
  media selection, article generation, duplicate handling, text extraction,
  and embedding do not covary with return distance strongly enough to create
  or reverse the sign of the latent association.
\end{assumption}

This premise links reported content to economic characteristics.
The firm-specific kernel $K_i$ then maps those characteristics to exposure
law $P_i$ under the transmission conditions in \Cref{ass:random-common-map}.
The tolerance $\tau_i$ measures departure from the common economic response;
measurement distortion is addressed separately by \Cref{ass:article-proxy}.

The distributions pool persistent attributes and transient events over the
sample window, providing a common-window summary of reported firm information.
The 10-K comparison in \Cref{sec:classification-controls} assesses how this
news geometry relates to firms' descriptions of their own businesses.

We use Qwen3-Embedding-8B for the headline article representation
\citep{qwen3_embedding_2025}.
Under pinned serving parameters, each full article body $d$ maps to a vector
$\bm{e}_d=\phi(d)\in\R^{\ppvalue{embed-dim}}$, an atom of
$\widehat Q_i$ in equation~\ref{eq:empirical-embedding-measure}.
Holding article counts fixed keeps the number and weight of atoms comparable
across firms rather than combining semantic comparisons with unequal
empirical support sizes.
Within each firm, articles are ordered lexicographically by their
stable URL hash.
All full-width encoder comparisons use the first $m=128$ articles under this
ordering; for the headline encoder we additionally use $m\in\{32,64,128\}$.
Let $x_{ia}$ denote the row-normalized embedding of selected article $a$, and
let $S_m$ be the set of permutations of $\{1,\ldots,m\}$.
The primary statistic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-wasserstein-2}
  \widehat W_{2,m}(i,j)
  = \left(\min_{\pi\in S_m}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{a=1}^{m}
  \norm{x_{ia}-x_{j,\pi(a)}}_2^{2}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The minimization pairs every article from one firm with one article from the
other and finds the smallest root-mean-square semantic displacement, so W$_2$
is small when two firms' article topics can be aligned closely even if no
individual article is shared.
The fixed-W$_2$ diagnostic uses the common $m=128$ unit-chord Qwen3
representation.
The complete W$_1$ and energy definitions are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:representation-diagnostics} as alternative distance summaries.

\subsection{Distance association and inference}
\label{sec:testable-restrictions}
\label{sec:primary-estimator}\label{sec:empirical-testing}
\label{sec:inference-design}

We regress return distance on article-embedding distance after absorbing
stable firm-level heterogeneity.
Its outcome is the return chord distance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord-distance}
  \hat{D}_{ij} = \sqrt{2(1 - \hat{\rho}_{ij})},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\rho}_{ij}$ is the sample Pearson correlation of daily returns
(equation~\ref{eq:sample-correlation} in \Cref{sec:data-provenance}).
On this scale, $D_{ij}=0$ denotes perfectly correlated returns,
$D_{ij}=\sqrt{2}$ denotes zero correlation, and $D_{ij}=2$ denotes perfectly
negative correlation.
Larger chord distance therefore means weaker return co-movement.

The regression is defined over the observed undirected pairs $i<j$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-fixed-effects}
  D_{ij}=\mu+a_i+a_j+\beta_X X_{ij}+z_{ij}^{\top}\gamma+u_{ij},
  \qquad 1\le i<j\le N.
\end{equation}
Here $N=\ppvalue{n-firms}$, $\mu$ is the intercept, $X_{ij}$ is the
article-embedding regressor with coefficient $\beta_X$, $z_{ij}$ is the
vector of secondary controls with coefficient vector $\gamma$, and $u_{ij}$
is the dyadic error.
The terms $a_i+a_j$ are symmetric additive firm effects.
The restriction $i<j$ counts each unordered pair once.
The normalization omits one firm-effect indicator; changing the omitted
ticker does not change $\widehat\beta_X$.
The canonical specification sets $X_{ij}=W^{(2)}_{ij}$ and $z_{ij}=0$,
yielding the fixed-W$_2$ slope $\beta_{W_2}$.
To test incremental association beyond classifications in this same
firm-effects design, we set $z_{ij}=S_{ij}$, where $S_{ij}$ indicates that
the two firms share a sector.
A finer adjustment sets $z_{ij}=(S_{ij},I_{ij})^{\top}$, where $I_{ij}$
indicates a shared industry, and a within-industry regression retains only
pairs with $I_{ij}=1$ and absorbs symmetric firm effects.
In each case, partial $R^2$ measures the reduction in residual sum of
squares from adding W$_2$ to a restricted model retaining all other controls.
For example, the sector-adjusted baseline includes both firm effects and
$S_{ij}$.

\begin{hypothesis}[Reduced-form distance association]
  \label{hyp:monotonicity}
  For the theory-aligned arm, the reported two-sided regression test is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic-association-hypothesis}
    H_0:\beta_{W_2}=0
    \qquad\text{against}\qquad
    H_1:\beta_{W_2}\ne0.
  \end{equation}
  We separately hypothesize $\beta_{W_2}>0$: greater article distance is
  associated with greater return distance after symmetric firm effects.
  The covariance envelope motivates this question; the sign of the average
  association is an empirical hypothesis.
\end{hypothesis}

Dyads sharing a firm may be dependent, so the cross-sectional information is
governed by the number of firms rather than the number of pairs.
Firms are therefore the resampling units, and symmetric additive firm effects
absorb stable endpoint heterogeneity.
Each multinomial node-bootstrap draw assigns a count to every firm and
weights dyad $(i,j)$ by the product of its endpoint counts to account for
dependence among pairs sharing a firm.
Percentile intervals and the finite-draw-corrected two-sided sign tail use
the same 1,999-draw schedule for every reported specification.

The residual representation, partial-$R^2$ definition, nuisance-rank details,
six-rung estimator audit, exact weights, rank checks, functional-form
diagnostics, and influence results are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:dyadic-diagnostics}.

A later-window specification holds the
\ppvalue{sample-year-start}--\ppvalue{sample-year-end} article-embedding
W$_2$ matrix fixed and rebuilds return chord distances and symmetric firm
effects for the 2023--2026 complete-case panel.
It uses the same dyads, estimator, bootstrap schedule, and two-sided null,
measuring persistence of the association across return windows.
The features are constructed retrospectively; encoder and classification
timing are documented in \Cref{sec:data-provenance}.

\subsection{Covariance sensitivity}\label{sec:covariance-sensitivity}

The covariance envelope provides a second use of article distance:
assessing candidate covariances under specified transmission conditions.
We apply the return bridge in \Cref{sec:methodology}, expressing exposures
and residuals in standardized total-return units.
Then $\rho_{ij}=\kappa_\pi$, but a firm's unit return variance is
$v_i+\sigma_{e_i}^2=1$, where $\sigma_{e_i}^2$ is its residual variance.
Let $q_{ij}:=\sigma_{e_i}^2+\sigma_{e_j}^2\ge0$.
Writing $\Delta_{ij}$ for the pair's realized transport excess, subtracting
optimal exposure cost from observed return distance gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total-return-excess-correction}
  H_{ij}:=c^{\mathrm{actual}}_{ij}-W_{2,\Gamma}^2(P_i,P_j)
  =\Delta_{ij}+q_{ij},
  \qquad c^{\mathrm{actual}}_{ij}:=2-2\rho_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Residual variance contributes additively to this diagnostic.
The transmission bracket gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:random-transport-excess-bracket}
  c^{\mathrm{actual}}_{ij}-(w^+_{ij})^2
  \le H_{ij}
  \le c^{\mathrm{actual}}_{ij}-(w^-_{ij})^2.
\end{equation}
We classify an interval as \emph{nonnegative} when its lower endpoint is
nonnegative, as a \emph{violation} when its upper endpoint is negative, and
as \emph{unresolved} when it spans zero.
Since $H_{ij}=\Delta_{ij}+q_{ij}\ge0$ under the joint measurement,
transmission, and return conditions, a negative upper endpoint flags
inconsistency in the plug-in scenario for the pair.
Recovering an interval for systematic excess $\Delta_{ij}$ additionally
requires subtracting residual variance $q_{ij}$ from both endpoints.

For this numerical scenario, we identify characteristic distance $G_{ij}$
with the Qwen3 $m=128$ article distance at the declared normalization and
impose the transmission restrictions on exposures in standardized-return units.
This numerical identification is additional to the association-level
measurement premise in \Cref{ass:article-proxy}.
The calculation uses estimated return moments
$c^{\mathrm{actual}}_{ij}=2-2\hat\rho_{ij}$.
The nine-cell grid varies multiplicative distortion
$L\in\{1,1.25,1.5\}$ and firm-level slack
$\tau_i=\tau_j\in\{0,0.05,0.10\}$ around the isometric, zero-slack benchmark.
These scenario inputs determine how much transmission uncertainty the
covariance restriction admits.

\subsection{Representation sensitivity}\label{sec:representation-ladder}

Representation checks ask which measurement choices sustain the distance
association, holding the firm sample, return matrix, estimator, and bootstrap
schedule fixed.
Supplementary lexical comparisons use firm-level TF--IDF cosine distance
and W$_2$ between distributions of TF--IDF article vectors.
Four additional W$_2$ comparisons use base encoders pretrained with causal
or masked language modeling; these supplementary comparisons use only
2018--2022 returns (\Cref{tab:text-controls}).
Qwen capacity variants compare model size, including a comparison at a common
output width; BGE changes the encoder architecture and training pipeline.
Nested leading-coordinate representations, trained to remain usable at
smaller widths through Matryoshka representation learning, assess how
compression changes the measured relationship.
W$_1$ substitutes proportional for quadratic transport cost, while energy
provides a different summary of the same article distributions.
The published ChronoBERT checkpoints for 2017--2024 assess sensitivity to the
encoder's reported training-information cutoff within a common architecture
\citep{he_chronobert_2025}.
Later checkpoints continue training earlier ones, so these are cumulative
annual-training comparisons, not matched-compute causal tests of leakage.
\Cref{sec:representation-diagnostics} gives construction details, paired
contrasts and their benchmark-calibrated equivalence rule, and
article-sampling checks.
